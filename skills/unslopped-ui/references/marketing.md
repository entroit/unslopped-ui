# Persuasive pages and visual direction

Read this for landing pages, portfolios and a requested new identity. For a page added to an existing product, use the existing identity and choose the new page's composition within it.

## Start with content and evidence

Identify the audience, concrete offer, proof available and next useful action. Write the information hierarchy before choosing a hero layout. A heading should explain the product or establish a deliberate editorial premise. Supporting copy should add information.

Use real product output, supplied imagery or a clearly identified concept where it helps explain the offer. Customer logos, testimonials, outcomes, availability, prices and technical claims require evidence. A decorative product mockup must not impersonate a working feature or an actual customer result.

## Choose a coherent direction

When the brief leaves the identity open, consider materially different compositions before committing. Change the relationship between content, type and media, not just the accent hue. Select one direction that fits the subject and the user's preferences. A suitable familiar layout is valid.

Record the decisions needed to keep subsequent sections coherent: type roles, palette roles, page grid, image treatment and one recurring visual device if it serves the identity. An existing brand does not need novelty for its own sake.

Use the catalog when a particular material, style or measured example helps resolve a choice. Retrieve the selected recipe and its qualifications. Large editorial type, serif display, cream, violet, sharp corners, glass or a wordmark footer can all be deliberate choices. Their usefulness depends on composition, legibility and the brief.

## Adapt the composition

Give each section a reason to exist and a clear relationship to its neighbors. Vary structure when the content changes; keep shared alignment and rhythm. A repeated grid is useful for genuinely comparable items. A composed blank area can support a photograph or statement, but should not hide the offer or make a phone screen appear unfinished.

Translate the defining composition to narrow screens. Preserve content order, readable text and the action. Oversized display text needs inspection with the real words, glyphs and language. Keep important content available if animation fails or reduced motion is requested.

Inspect images at their actual crop. Retrieval tags, dominant colors and catalog quality scores are selection hints. Verify the selected image's source, credit and permitted use. A stock photograph does not document the user's own staff, premises or customers.
